Recap Launch night

Revuelto,
under the lights.

The cover comes off in front of the people who will own them — which is the only audience that has ever mattered for a car like this.

A reveal is a strange piece of theatre. The car has already been photographed from every angle, written about for months and argued over on the internet by people who will never sit in one. And still, when the lights drop in a room in North Miami Beach and the cover comes off, everybody leans forward.

Part of that is the room. A launch at the factory is a press event; a launch here is a client event, and the difference shows in who is standing at the rope. These are people who already have two or three of them in a garage twenty minutes away, and who will spend the next hour asking about carbon options rather than horsepower.

What the photographs miss

Pictures of a car under show lighting tell you about the paint and nothing about the shape. The Revuelto is a difficult car to photograph honestly: it reads flat in a three-quarter shot and only makes sense when you walk the length of it and watch the line over the rear haunch change as you move. Nobody who has only seen it on a screen has seen it.

The second thing a photograph cannot carry is the noise, or in this case the absence of it. The first few metres are electric, and a room that has spent thirty years learning to expect a V12 clearing its throat gets something quieter and considerably faster instead. The reaction is always the same: a pause, then a laugh.

Afterwards

The useful part of an evening like this happens after the presentation, when the lights come up and the room turns into a showroom again. Specification conversations start at the rear wheel and end at a desk. That is the whole point of doing it here rather than sending clients to a hotel ballroom.

The car stayed on the floor for the rest of the week. Several of the people in these photographs came back on their own, without an appointment, to look at it again in daylight.
